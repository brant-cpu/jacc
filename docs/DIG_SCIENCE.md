# Dig site science and model limits

This is an original side-view training model, with no copied arcade characters, artwork or combat. Visuals and depth are schematic and not to scale. The site uses automatic excavation and fictional support lifts, has no collapse/air/heat/death mechanic, and does not teach real excavation procedures.

## Content distinctions

- Quartz is a mineral; amethyst is a purple quartz variety. Calcite is calcium carbonate. Granite and basalt are rocks rather than single minerals. “Gem” describes valued material/use, not an independent mineral class. The iron-bearing find uses magnetite as an example rather than declaring all iron ores identical.
- Fossils are preserved remains/traces of ancient life. They stay in the journal and are excluded from construction input selections.
- Some cave-adapted animals have reduced eyes or pigmentation. Reduced pigmentation is not automatically albinism; albinism affects melanin production. Cave animals are not universally blind. Many bats use both vision and echolocation.
- Stalactites hang from ceilings; stalagmites grow from floors. Dripping water can deposit calcite. Real growth rates vary and can be very slow; the diagram is not a real-time growth simulation.
- Groundwater fills pores/fractures. The water table is the top of a saturated zone, not necessarily a cavern lake. Recharge can change it. The pictured levels are illustrative, not real water-supply predictions.
- Underground molten rock is magma; at the surface it is lava. Magma can occur in the crust. The mantle is mostly solid rock that deforms over long times, not a global lava ocean. Ordinary caves do not reach the mantle: the Deep Earth view is explicitly a PIP cutaway model, not a place the astronaut physically drills into.
- Construction workbenches are sample-guided sandbox models. Ore needs refining, glass requires processing, and real buildings/water channels need design/testing. A clicked quartz sample is not claimed to turn instantly into a structurally qualified glass panel.
