## Proposed 2D biome learning framework

Primary placement: a biome comparison station in the existing Planet Farm biodome. It connects to Sumati's ecosystems/environmental science tools, Lucas's organism/adaptation tools and future My World/My Solar System planning. A separate biome region is not yet being added.

The supplied screenshots list seven biome categories: Rainforest, Grassland, Coniferous Forest, Temperate Deciduous Forest, Desert, Tundra and Shrubland. This records the displayed classification, not an exhaustive or universal biome classification.

- Tap a habitat to explore an illustrated 2D scene.
- Observe model climate patterns, water availability, vegetation and animal adaptations. Explain that biomes reflect long-term conditions; one rainy day does not change a desert into a forest.
- Compare two model settings using discrete taps, with PIP explaining changes. Avoid presenting simplified diagrams as ecological predictions.
- Keep wildlife observations in the journal without capturing animals or requiring quizzes.
- Connect farm/habitat engineering to appropriate conditions. Future fictional worlds must distinguish Earth examples, speculative environments and evidence for life.

Sumati studies relationships among organisms and surroundings; Lucas studies organisms and biological adaptations. All astronauts can use the activities.

### Proposed J.A.C.C. activity: CubeSat Cleanroom

**Placement:** engineering/construction zone, linked to the HQ science lab and a future launch pad. It is a construction/science activity, not a separate new gameplay mode. Status: planned, not implemented.

1. Choose a pictured science mission: observe clouds/rain, look at stars, or relay data. Present different mission needs explicitly.
2. Tap parts to examine computer/data handling, power, communications, attitude control, navigation and payload. Propulsion is mission-dependent; many CubeSats have none.
3. Tap a test station. The astronaut/PIP automatically carries and positions the selected item. Tests show a simple illustration of what is being checked.
4. Tap a frame slot to fit a tested part; placement snaps into position. PIP can suggest a useful next step or demonstrate it. Parts can be removed and changed without loss.
5. Check the assembled training model's mass and vacuum-readiness demonstration. These are simplified educational checks, not real flight qualification.
6. Save the design in the journal and transfer it to a launch-preparation display. Actual launches, orbital mission data and deployment to My Solar System are future connections, not existing functionality.

Guided Adventure gives one optional step at a time. Free Explore lets children revisit stations and experiment. No countdown, quotas, competitive scores or required multitasking. Multiple projects can be a later optional feature; a single project must remain sufficient. Provide large single-tap choices, named controls, accessible destination selection, spoken explanations/captions, undo/restart and switch/controller access. Use shapes, symbols and names as well as part colors.

### Science details to refine before production

- A 1U CubeSat is approximately 10 × 10 × 10 cm (about four inches per side). Multi-unit spacecraft need not be cubes, and CubeSat formats extend beyond 6U. Use the relevant current deployer specification before teaching exact dimensions or limits.
- Reaction wheels control orientation (attitude); they are not car steering wheels and do not directly steer a spacecraft's orbital path.
- Star trackers measure attitude against star patterns; they do not independently supply full orbital position/navigation. Distinguish orientation, position estimation and trajectory changes.
- Batteries store energy; solar panels generate electrical power from sunlight. Mission design determines the required systems and payload.
- Vacuum and mass checks are only some of the engineering tests real spacecraft require. Do not present the activity as a full launch-safety checklist.

Aiden introduces assembly; Malik computer/data systems; Alex power/communications concepts; Kaito attitude and flight; Lilja measurement; Sumati Earth-observation goals; and the blue Sagan astronaut astronomy payloads once his identity is complete. These are optional introductions, not exclusive ability gates. All crew can build.

### Proposed J.A.C.C. activity: Current Workshop

**Placement:** Aquatic Learning Center, in the ocean simulation alongside buoyancy, salinity and oceanography models. Do not add salt/heat to the actual freshwater home lake. Status: planned, not implemented.

- A simple 2D profile grid shows a training submarine, current arrows, obstacles and a discovery capsule.
- Select a model tool, then a large tile. Use a tool button to turn arrows; no dragging is required. Provide labeled tile choices for switch/controller/screen-reader access, not just spatial pointing.
- Tools model a denser downward-flow region, a warmer upward-flow region, a lateral pump/flow deflector and a barrier. Explicitly label these as controls in a teaching model, not literal tools for steering the real ocean.
- Run the model and the submarine follows automatically. Offer Pause, one-step advance, Undo, Reset, PIP demonstration and optional narration/captions. No oxygen countdown, penalty for a blocked route or timed selection. A blocked route simply invites another design.
- Guided play introduces one tool/concept at a time; Free Explore lets children change the model and repeat. A key/discovery capsule can reveal a reusable journal illustration or observation, with no currency or competitive score.
- Later connect satellite temperature/salinity observations to the planned CubeSat activity, and habitat/water-system designs to farming and future aquatic worlds. These cross-zone systems are future work.

### Science boundaries and corrections

At comparable temperature and pressure, saltier water is generally denser; warming seawater generally reduces density at fixed salinity/pressure. Sinking or rising depends on density relative to surrounding water, with temperature, salinity and pressure all relevant. Do not teach that adding salt or heat always creates a particular full-ocean current.

Sea-ice formation largely rejects salt into surrounding seawater; real sea ice can retain brine. Melting adds freshwater. Cold meltwater does **not** inevitably sink: its lower salinity can make it buoyant despite its lower temperature. Freshwater also has a density maximum near 4 °C; avoid applying a universal warm-up/rise rule to all water.

The global overturning circulation is more complex than a single conveyor loop. Wind, Earth's rotation, mixing, coastlines and seafloor shape also influence currents. Avoid the supplied text's categorical claims about climates freezing or overheating without a single circulation mechanism.

SMAP uses microwave observations to infer sea-surface salinity; it does not directly measure every current or deep-ocean salinity. Verify mission-specific details against authoritative sources before production. Treat exact numerical model parameters as configurable educational approximations requiring science review.
