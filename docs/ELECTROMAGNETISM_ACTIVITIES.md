## Strong first stations

1. **Field Viewer:** static magnetic arrows, poles and compasses, with a clear distinction between a field diagram and actual physical lines.
2. **Magnetic Crane:** power and coil-turn comparisons, optional material tests and links to engineering robots.
3. **Generator Bench:** move a model magnet through a coil in discrete steps and compare changing-flux readings.
4. **Spectrum Explorer:** radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma categories; every band has labels, patterns and an optional spoken description.
5. **Wave Comparison Table:** compare sound and electromagnetic waves, including propagation with/without a medium.
6. **Star Fingerprint Lab:** compare simplified absorption/emission line patterns and connect to future CubeSat astronomy instruments.

These priorities are recommendations for this collection, not replacements for the owner's other activity priorities.

## Scientific precision

Electromagnetism concerns electric and magnetic fields and their interactions with charge and currents. Current produces magnetic fields; changing magnetic flux through a circuit can induce an electromotive force. A coil wound around a nail and connected to a battery demonstrates an electromagnet, not induction by itself. A sustained current in an induced circuit also depends on its electrical connections.

Microwaves are commonly treated as a subset of radio waves; category boundaries depend on convention. The familiar seven-band spectrum is a teaching organization, not seven unrelated physical substances. In vacuum, electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed, with frequency and wavelength inversely related. Photon energy increases with frequency, but beam power/intensity is a separate quantity. Do not teach that a higher-frequency source must always heat an object more strongly.

Visible light is only a small portion of the electromagnetic spectrum. Infrared is not synonymous with all heat transfer. Microwave heating depends on interaction with materials; avoid simplistic claims that all molecules respond identically. Sound requires a material medium, while electromagnetic waves can propagate through vacuum.

Magnetic field lines visualize direction and field structure; they are not literal threads. Outside a bar magnet they conventionally point from north to south and form closed loops through the magnet. Not all metals are strongly attracted to magnets; use scientifically reviewed example materials and avoid identifying all iron-bearing minerals as magnetic.

In a simple coil model more current/turns can strengthen the field, but real cores, geometry, saturation and heating limit behavior. Illustrative forces must not be presented as calibrated engineering predictions. Numerical readings need explicit model assumptions and units.

Star chemistry is inferred from spectral features with appropriate models and context, not simply from a star's visible color. Use schematic line patterns as labeled examples until validated instrument/species data is available.

## Accessible interaction standard

All experiments use select → change one setting → observe → compare → repeat. Provide single-tap controls, snapping placement, no required dragging/holding/timed responses, switch/controller selection, optional narration/captions and PIP demonstrations. No activity is a mandatory quiz. Escape-room concepts remain optional mysteries with a permanently available exit.

Avoid flashing spectrum effects, strobing fields or mandatory loud signals. Use labels, shapes and patterns alongside color, preserve sensory/display settings and keep AAC accessible. Link observations to the journal when implemented. Original art, scientific review, real-device testing and child/accessibility testing remain required.
