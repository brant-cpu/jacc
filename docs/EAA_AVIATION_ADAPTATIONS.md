## Shared interaction pattern

Choose a model → change one setting → Run → observe → compare → repeat. Every required action uses a single tap or its keyboard/switch/controller equivalent. Travel, positioning, launches, drops and measurements are automatic. Offer Pause, Replay, Step, PIP demonstration and Undo/Reset where appropriate. There are no reflex tests, racing opponents, scores or mandatory quizzes. Guided play gives one optional prompt at a time; exploration allows revisiting individual stations freely.

Use original schematic art, large targets and named alternatives to spatial pointing. Force directions, selected states and graph series need labels/shapes/patterns as well as color. Demonstrations can slow down or advance by steps without making timing part of success. Keep existing AAC, captions and sensory preferences available.

## Science details to refine

- Lift is perpendicular to the relative airflow; drag opposes motion relative to air; weight acts toward the attracting body. Their simple up/down/forward/back layout applies only to the selected training scenario, not every maneuver.
- Balanced net force means zero acceleration, not necessarily zero speed. Unbalanced forces change velocity; they do not automatically imply a particular steady flight path without further assumptions.
- Do not teach that lift requires a curved upper wing or that air parcels must reunite at the trailing edge. Wing angle, shape and airflow affect pressure and momentum changes. Pressure explanations and downward air deflection describe compatible aspects of the same interaction.
- A launcher provides an initial impulse. A coasting paper glider does not retain engine thrust after release. Distance also depends on mass, drag, launch conditions and design.
- Pitch, roll and yaw are rotations about aircraft axes, not simply upward/downward translation or compulsory level turns. Ailerons mainly control roll, elevators pitch and the rudder yaw. Real control arrangements and coupled motions vary.
- Flaps generally change both lift and drag; do not reproduce the source's statement that they universally move only down or function solely as brakes.
- Mass and weight differ. Parachutes, aerodynamic wings and ordinary paper rotors require an atmosphere. Contrast Earth flight with Moon/vacuum examples accurately; reaction thrust can work in vacuum.
- Numerical results need a defined, tested model and appropriate units. Simplified arrows/animations are not validated CFD, aircraft performance predictions or instructions for real flight.

## Recommended first implementations

Start with the Parachute Drop Tower, Balloon Rocket Rail and Snap-Together Airplane. They support clear one-variable comparisons, straightforward 2D art and automatic motion. Then extend the already planned Paper-Airplane Launcher and add the more involved Wind Tunnel/force-balance activities. These recommendations do not supersede the owner's broader activity priorities.

Actual numerical models, scientific review, accessible user testing, GDevelop rendering and real-device validation are still required. Source review alone does not mean the activities are built or scientifically approved for production.
