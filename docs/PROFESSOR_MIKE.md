# Professor Mike — J.A.C.C. character profile

Professor Mike is the warm, clever instructor of the Robotics Lab and Computer Lab at GSEI HQ. He is a fictional character inspired by the creator’s friend Mike Dowd and his engineering and game-making spirit.

Creative reference supplied by the creator: Mike Dowd was a software engineer who worked on rocket systems at Whisper, SpaceX and Rocket Lab, later worked at the AI company Magenta, and loved games. These notes inform the fictional instructor’s interests; they are not Professor Mike’s literal employment history.

## Personality

- **Curious and inventive:** explores puzzles one clue at a time.
- **Playful and strategic:** enjoys games, clever solutions and a little friendly mischief.
- **Patient and encouraging:** treats bugs and unexpected results as useful clues.
- **Quietly funny:** uses dry, gentle humor children can understand.
- **Believes in the player:** notices children’s ideas and encourages their own solutions.

## Teaching and companion roles

Professor Mike introduces a coding or robotics challenge and explains its purpose briefly. He offers manageable steps and celebrates experimentation, including unexpected results. His challenges can draw on rocket software and AI concepts.

**PIP stays with the child as the active helper.** Mike is the subject instructor. PIP clarifies what is happening, offers contextual help, demonstrates when invited, and supports the next action. Malik and Kaito remain astronaut teammates and makers alongside the player.

Use this pattern throughout J.A.C.C.: each zone has its own subject instructor; PIP is the consistent companion. Other zone instructors remain unassigned until established by the creator. Professor Elon remains HQ and J.A.C.C. program director.

Directions stay short, friendly and readable aloud. Activities support taps, repetition and independent exploration without timers, pressure, forced tutorials or mandatory reading. PIP offers one small step rather than a list of demands. Mike never treats a child’s unexpected choice as personal failure.

## Sample line

“Cool, the robot followed our instructions exactly. That means our instructions need a tiny tune-up. PIP, want to help us try one change?”

Use the full line as a writing reference. Small on-screen cards can split or shorten it while retaining the handoff to PIP.
